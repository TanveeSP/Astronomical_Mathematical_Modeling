## 🔹 Q1: Lunar Months in One Year

This problem determines how many **lunar months** fit into one **Earth year**.

A lunar month is approximately **29.53 days**, while a solar year is **365 days**.
By dividing the total days in a year by the lunar cycle, we estimate the number of lunar months.

**Result Insight:**
This highlights the mismatch between **lunar and solar calendars**, which is why calendar adjustments (like leap months) are needed.

👉 [Open Notebook](./Q1.ipynb)

---

## 🔹 Q2: Distance of a Geostationary Satellite

A geostationary satellite remains fixed relative to Earth, meaning its orbital period equals Earth's rotation.

Using Newton’s gravitational law combined with circular motion, the orbital radius is calculated.

**Approach:**

* Use Earth’s rotation period (24 hours)
* Apply gravitational formula
* Solve for orbital radius

**Result Insight:**
We obtain the satellite’s distance from Earth, which lies in the **geostationary orbit region (~36,000 km above Earth)**.

👉 [Open Notebook](./Q2.ipynb)

## 🔹 Q3: Moon Recession vs Universe Expansion

The Moon is moving away from Earth at a very small rate (~3.8 cm/year).
This is compared with the expansion of the universe using **Hubble’s Law**.

**Approach:**

* Convert lunar recession into comparable units
* Compare with cosmic expansion rate

**Result Insight:**
Shows that **cosmic expansion dominates at large scales**, while local systems behave differently.

👉 [Open Notebook](./Q3.ipynb)
